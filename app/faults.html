Faults and protection trips

The ACOM 1010 watches its own currents, its reflected power and its tuning, and steps in before damage is done. This page explains every warning and trip: what the panel shows, what it means, and what to do. It also covers the operator mistakes the amplifier cannot always catch for you.

1. Two levels of protection

When the amplifier detects an abnormal condition, its controller weighs the risk and applies one of two degrees of protection (Manual §4.6):

  • A warning. A yellow LED lights: G1 (control grid), G2 (screen grid) or IP (plate current). You may keep operating, but a trip is likely to follow. Treat it as a request to back off now.
  • A trip. The red F (fault) LED lights, the green OPER LED goes off, and the amplifier drops to STANDBY for several seconds. Other LEDs say why. Then AUTO-OPERATE returns it to OPERATE by itself (Manual §4.2).

The automatic return is a convenience, not permission to carry on. If it trips repeatedly, stop and find the cause, which is usually too much drive or an antenna mismatch (Manual §4.6).

3. Each indication

G1 Control-grid overload Manual §3.2j

What it is
The tube's control grid, the electrode the drive signal goes to, is drawing current. In this amplifier it should draw almost none. Grid current means the drive has pushed the grid past the point the tube is designed for.
Usual cause
Too much drive from the transceiver. About 60 W gives rated output (Manual §1.3), and anything well beyond that only overdrives the tube.
Do this
Reduce the drive power (Manual §3.2j).
If it trips
F and G1 light together, and the amplifier goes to STANDBY for several seconds.

G2 Screen-grid overload Manual §3.2j

What it is
The 4CX800A is a tetrode: between its control grid and its plate is a second grid, the screen, held at a few hundred volts. When the plate's voltage swing comes down close to the screen voltage, the screen starts catching electrons meant for the plate. Its current climbs fast, and the screen has only a small power rating.
Usual cause
Too much drive, or tuning that leaves the plate-load resistance too high, so the plate swing runs out of room. That is LOAD too far to the left, with the TRI showing left.
Do this
Reduce drive and/or retune: peak FWD with TUNE and center the TRI with LOAD (Manual §3.2j, §4.5).
If it trips
F and G2 light together.

IP Plate-current overload Manual §3.2j

What it is
The tube is drawing more plate current than it should. More current means more power in and more heat in the tube.
Usual cause
Too much drive, or tuning that leaves the plate-load resistance too low (LOAD too far right, TRI showing right). The manual's service checks expect no more than about 600 mA in SSB and CW (Manual §5.6).
Do this
Reduce drive and/or retune (Manual §3.2j, §4.5).
If it trips
F and IP light together.

F + RFL Reflected-power trip Manual §4.5a, §4.6

What it is
Too much power is coming back from the antenna system. The amplifier is safe only while reflected power stays below 250 W (Manual §4.5a). When it gets there, the last red LED of the RFL bar and the F LED light.
Usual cause
An antenna mismatch at high power: the wrong antenna selected for the band, an antenna not built for the band, a damaged antenna or feedline, or ice and wind changing the antenna. Section 4 shows how SWR limits power.
Do this
Reduce drive, or improve the antenna's SWR (Manual §4.6). Check A1-A2 is right for the band: A1 for 10, 15 and 20 m, A2 for 40 and 80 m at W3USR. Retuning the amplifier does not fix SWR (see the Smith chart tutorial).

F + TRI flashing Tuning trip Manual §4.6

What it is
All three TRI LEDs flash together with F. The amplifier has judged that the tuning is not adjusted correctly, or that the antenna's impedance has changed, and it is not safe to keep driving the tube that way.
Usual cause
Driving hard before tuning, a BAND switch that does not match the transceiver's band, knobs far from the Table 4-1 presets, or an antenna that changed (a different antenna selected, weather, a fault).
Do this
Retune at low drive (Manual §4.5): check BAND and the antenna, preset from Table 4-1, then 10–20 W key-down, peak FWD with TUNE and center the TRI with LOAD. The Pi network and Smith chart tutorials explain what the knobs are doing.

F alone Manual §3.2j

What it is
A trip with none of the cause LEDs lit.
Do this
Check the keying wiring from the transceiver to the amplifier's KEY-IN connector (Manual §3.2j, §2.4d).

STOP All bar-graph LEDs flashing Manual §4.6

What it is
A serious fault inside the amplifier.
Do this
Switch the amplifier off immediately to avoid damage (Manual §4.6), and tell the station trustee. Do not open the amplifier: it holds up to 3,000 V, which is lethal. The manual requires unplugging it and waiting at least 30 minutes before the cover comes off (Manual §5); leave that to a trained technician.

4. How SWR limits your power

The reflected fraction is |Γ|², the square of the reflection coefficient, and depends only on the SWR (see the Smith chart tutorial on |Γ| versus |Γ|²). Since reflected power must stay under 250 W, the SWR sets the most forward power you can run. The manual's examples: at 2.5:1, 800 W forward comes with 150 W reflected; at 5:1, the most forward power you can get is about 540 W, with 240 W reflected and only 300 W reaching the antenna system (Manual §4.5a).

5. Operator mistakes the trips do not cover

Some mistakes damage the amplifier without any trip to warn you. They are the operator's responsibility, and the trainer treats each one as unsafe: in a band-change sprint, any of them disqualifies the run.

Never Turning BAND while transmitting Manual §3.2h

Hot switching will eventually destroy the band switch, and the warranty does not cover it. Unkey first, every time.

Never Pressing A1-A2 while transmitting Manual §4.4

Changing antennas while transmitting can cause damage the warranty does not cover (Manual §4.4). Select the antenna before you key.

Never Pressing RTTY while transmitting Manual §4.3

Change to or from RTTY mode, or any other mode, only between transmissions. RTTY mode itself needs no retuning; it limits output to 500 W for continuous-duty modes.

Never More than one minute of continuous drive while tuning Manual §4.5

Key down for at most a minute, then pause at least a minute so the tube can cool. The trainer's transmit button shows a timer and turns amber after 45 seconds.

Never Transmitting with no antenna or dummy load connected Manual §4.4

Do not transmit into an antenna output unless it is connected by suitable coax to an antenna or a dummy load. An open output reflects nearly everything; try "Open coax" in the Smith chart tutorial.

Wait Transmitting during warm-up Manual §4.1

For about 150 seconds after power-on, the OPER LED flashes and the amplifier stays in STANDBY. The drive then passes straight through to the antenna at transceiver power. It does no harm, but nothing is being amplified yet, and it is not tuning. Wait for a steady OPER LED.

6. After a trip

  1. Unkey. Stop transmitting.
  2. Read the panel while the fault is still shown: which LED is lit with F? Fault information stays up for several seconds (Manual §4.6).
  3. Fix the cause before keying again: less drive, the right antenna, the right band, or a retune at 10–20 W.
  4. Let AUTO-OPERATE bring it back. The OPER LED comes back on by itself (Manual §4.2).
  5. If it trips again, stop. Repeated trips mean the cause is still there. Ask the station trustee before going on.
